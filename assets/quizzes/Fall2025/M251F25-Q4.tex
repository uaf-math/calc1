\problem{10} The \emph{derivative} of $f(x)$ is defined as follows: $$\ds f'(x)=\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}.$$
\begin{subproblems}
\item Use the definition of the derivative (above) to find the derivative of the function $\ds f(x)= \frac{5}{x+2}.$ Use proper limit notation. No credit will be awarded for finding the derivative via other methods.\\
\vfill
\vfill
\item Use your answer in part (a) to find the \emph{slope} of the line tangent to $\ds f(x)= \frac{5}{x+2}$ at the point $(3,1)$. Simplify your answer.
\vfill
\end{subproblems}
